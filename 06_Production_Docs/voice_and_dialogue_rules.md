# Voice And Dialogue Rules

Dialogue is mandatory. The movie must never feel silent or boring.

## Every 30-Second Part Must Include

- At least 3 spoken lines.
- Prefer 4-6 short spoken lines if the action allows it.
- At least 2 different speaking voices when more than one character is present.
- Character speech first; narrator only supports context.
- Spoken dialogue must be natural American English.
- Lines must be short enough to fit inside 30 seconds.
- Dialogue should reveal emotion, story, or danger, not filler.

## Audio Priority

1. Character dialogue must be loudest and clearest.
2. Narrator voice is second priority.
3. Sound effects support action but never cover speech.
4. Music stays under voices.

## Dola Prompt Rule

Use exact `DIALOGUE` timing labels:

`DIALOGUE 1-3s: Lily (soft scared young American woman): "Somebody help me!"`

Do not write only visual action. Every part needs voice.

## Avoid

- Silent video.
- Long narration replacing character speech.
- Too many words for one timing window.
- Dialogue subtitles on screen.
- Robotic/exposition-only lines.

## Good Pattern For 30 Seconds

- 0-3s: Hook line.
- 4-10s: Context or reaction line.
- 11-18s: Conflict line.
- 19-25s: Emotional or action line.
- 26-30s: Punch/cliffhanger line.
